1. Project Introduction

Problem: Buildings vibrate because of earthquakes, traffic, machines and wind. Too much vibration can hurt structural safety and make people uncomfortable.

Objective: Analyze vibration data, find the dominant frequency, calculate key vibration values and show how a building moves.

What is building vibration? It is the back-and-forth shaking of a building. We measure it with acceleration (how quickly the shaking speed changes) over time.

7. Mathematical Concepts

RMS (Root Mean Square) gives the “overall strength” of shaking:

RMS = sqrt( mean( x² ) )

Square every value, take the average, then take the square root.

FFT (Fast Fourier Transform) converts a vibration signal from the time domain (value vs time) to the frequency domain (how much shaking at each frequency). The tallest peak is the dominant frequency.

Building model:

M x'' + K x = F(t)
  • M = mass matrix (how heavy each floor is)
  • K = stiffness matrix (how stiff the columns are)
  • x = displacement (how far floors move)
  • F(t) = external force (earthquake, wind, traffic…) changing with time

8. Eigenvalue / Eigenvector Connection

K φ = λ M φ  →  natural frequency f = sqrt(λ) / (2π)

Solving this gives eigenvalues (λ), which give natural frequencies, and eigenvectors (φ), which give mode shapes (the way floors move together). If the shaking frequency matches a natural frequency, the building shakes much more (resonance).

Live example: 2-storey building, each floor mass m = 1000 kg, stiffness k = 2,000,000 N/m per storey.
